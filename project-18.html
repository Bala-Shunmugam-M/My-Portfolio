The problem

Why this exists.

Campus reports can be duplicated, lose their history or arrive without a clear owner and deadline. The underlying business problem is how to model reports, incidents, people and actions so the organization can answer what happened and what should happen next.

The approach

How it responds.

CampusPulse divides the data platform from the compliance application. The campus directory and facility data live in one schema; case-management records live in another. The application turns reports into a visible case workflow with tenant context, deadlines and an audit trail.

  1. 01

    Model

    Define institutions, users, locations, assets, reports, incidents and their relationships.

  2. 02

    Constrain

    Use relational keys and business rules to preserve identity, tenancy and lifecycle integrity.

  3. 03

    Work

    Take a report through case handling with roles, status transitions and recorded actions.

  4. 04

    Analyze

    Use SQL and dashboards to inspect intake, case distribution and response measures.

What the evidence supports

Demonstrated, with context.

These views were captured from the running local application against a newly initialized PostgreSQL demo database. The seeded institutions and accounts are fictional. The captured institution shows ten synthetic reports and nine cases in several workflow states; dashboard response-time and case metrics describe that sample only. The original project database and its records were not used for these screenshots.

Scope & limitations

  • The web-application screenshots show a small seeded workflow. They are separate from the much larger synthetic facility dataset documented by the original data-platform README.
  • Schema separation, role controls and audit behavior are described from source. This portfolio run is not a fresh security audit or a certification of tenant isolation.
  • The public report entry screen, staff overview and case queue were captured. No real student reports or sensitive incident details are displayed.
  • Optional AI drafting and hosted deployment were not exercised. A working local prototype is not evidence of live institutional adoption.
