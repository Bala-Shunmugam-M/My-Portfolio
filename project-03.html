Sponge Iron Operations Study — Agni Steels

Field Study · Coal-Based DRI Rotary Kiln · 100 TPD

An observational operations study at Agni Steels' Sponge Iron Division in Perundurai, Tamil Nadu — a 100 TPD coal-based Direct Reduction (DRI) unit. Mapped the rotary kiln process flow, recorded real downtime patterns described by the operations team, and applied Pareto and Fishbone analysis to identify the dominant constraint: kiln accretion (ring formation).

01

The plant

Agni Steels Sponge Iron Division at Ingur, Perundurai (Erode District, Tamil Nadu). A 100 TPD coal-based rotary kiln DRI unit producing sponge iron captively for the company's billet and TMT bar operations. Vertically integrated: Sponge → SMS (Steel Melting Shop) → Rolling Mill.

02

My role

Operations Management intern, attached primarily to the sponge iron unit. Daily walkthroughs with the shift team, conversations with operators and the shift in-charge, time spent in the control room observing SCADA screens, and short visits to the billet casting area and rolling mill.

03

My approach

An observational case study — not a Six Sigma project with measured data. I mapped the sponge iron process flow, recorded the problems operators repeatedly mentioned, and applied Pareto + Fishbone frameworks to rank causes by frequency/impact.

04

What I took away

In a 100 TPD coal-based DRI plant, kiln accretion (ring formation) is the dominant operational constraint. Raw material consistency is the next-most-important lever. SCADA gives visibility but doesn't predict accretion — predictive analytics (ANN/CFD) is the obvious next frontier.

Process flow

Coal-based DRI — the rotary kiln process.

100 TPD
Standard production sequence

The actual production sequence in the sponge iron unit. Standard for a 100 TPD coal-based DRI plant, validated by what I observed on-site.

Raw material yard → storage & dispatch

— Six stages, as observed on the plant floor
01Raw material yard. Iron ore (hematite lumps, 5–18 mm, sourced from Karnataka and Kumba/South Africa) stockpiled alongside non-coking coal (premium South African and domestic). Dolomite as flux for desulphurisation. Reddish ore piles, dark coal piles, clearly separated.
02Material handling & preparation. Conveyor belts, crushers and sizing screens proportion the ore-coal-flux mix. Quality of sizing here directly affects bed permeability and downstream metallization.
03Rotary kiln — the main reactor. A long inclined rotating furnace (~42 m × 3 m typical for 100 TPD). Material flows counter-current to combustion gases. Two thermal zones: preheating (~800–1000 °C) → reduction (~1050–1150 °C). Reactions: C + CO₂ → 2CO, then Fe₂O₃ → FeO → Fe (metallic). Residence time 6–8 hours. · the core reaction, >1150 °C avoided to prevent ring formation
04Rotary cooler. Hot DRI from the kiln passes through an indirect water-cooled rotary cooler down to ~100–150 °C — low enough to prevent re-oxidation, safe enough for handling.
05Product separation & screening. Magnetic separators pull the metallic sponge iron from char, ash, and dolochar by-products. Vibrating screens sort by size (lumps vs. fines). Char is collected for resale or reuse.
06Storage & dispatch. Sponge iron lumps and fines stored in silos/bunkers, dispatched to the captive Steel Melting Shop (SMS). Separately, an Electrostatic Precipitator (ESP) handles kiln exhaust dust for emission control — environmental compliance, not a production stage.
Operators monitor the kiln-shell temperature profile via thermocouples; the reduction zone is the step everything else is built to protect — too hot, and ring-forming accretion follows.

Field observations

What I actually saw & heard.

Multiple shifts
Walkthroughs + operator conversations

No measured downtime data was logged for this study. Observations below are from direct walkthroughs of the sponge iron unit and conversations with operators, shift in-charges and process engineers over multiple shifts.

01 · Recurring issue
Kiln accretion

Kiln accretion / ring formation was the recurring issue

On at least two days during my time on-site, the rotary kiln had to be slowed down or partially shut to manage accretion build-up. Operators said this reduces effective kiln volume and pulls metallization down. The shift in-charge estimated, in a casual conversation, that kiln-related issues (accretion + refractory wear + feeder jamming) account for roughly 40–50% of downtime in the sponge unit. This is an operator estimate, not a logged figure.

02 · Lab QC
Metallization

Metallization in the 88–93% range

Daily lab reports I was shown indicated metallization targets of 88–91%, with 92–93% on good days. An engineer demonstrated how metallization dropped when kiln speed wasn't adjusted in proportion to feed rate.

03 · Raw materials
Variability

Raw material variability

Even with premium sourcing, operators mentioned that coal ash content varies batch-to-batch, and iron ore quality fluctuates enough to disturb feed-rate stability and the reducing-atmosphere chemistry.

04 · Control room
SCADA

Reduction-zone temperature excursions

SCADA screens in the control room sometimes showed reduction-zone temperatures pushing above 1150 °C — a threshold operators try to stay under to prevent ring formation.

05 · Downstream
Separation

Magnetic separator overload

On one shift, material queued at the product separation area because the magnetic separator was overloaded with char — a downstream symptom of a particular kiln run.

06 · Compliance
ESP maintenance

ESP maintenance event

The Electrostatic Precipitator was running, but one section was visibly under maintenance during my visit — confirming pollution-control upkeep is a parallel operational concern.

Analysis · Fishbone (6M)

Root causes of kiln accretion.

Ishikawa breakdown
Operator input + DRI literature

Fishbone (Ishikawa) breakdown of the root causes of kiln accretion, applying the 6M framework. Built from operator conversations and standard DRI process literature.

M

Material

Ash content variability in coal — high ash + low ash-fusion temperature → low-melting eutectics inside the kiln. Iron-ore gangue chemistry (SiO₂ / Al₂O₃) drives ring composition.

M

Method

Coal-to-iron ratio (FC/Fe) outside the 0.4–0.52 optimal window. Air injection profile not adjusted dynamically to feed quality changes.

M

Machine

Refractory wear shifts the kiln's effective inner diameter and heat transfer behaviour over time. Air-fan positions/flows degrade between maintenance windows.

M

Measurement

SCADA monitors shell temperature but cannot predict accretion. Metallization tested in lab — a lagging indicator. Gas analysis (CO/CO₂/H₂) is the closest leading indicator available.

M

Man (operator)

Manual setpoint adjustments depend on operator experience. Reduction-zone temperature occasionally seen above 1150 °C — the threshold for ring formation risk.

M

Environment

Ambient temperature and humidity affect combustion air density and kiln draft, which subtly shift the temperature profile over the course of a day.

Analysis · Recommendations

Improvements by horizon.

Framework expectations
Not measured plant outcomes

Improvement recommendations grouped by horizon. Numbers are framework expectations from DRI literature, not measured plant outcomes.

Immediate
Raw material QA

Strengthen raw material QA at intake

Tighten coal ash and ash-fusion-temperature acceptance criteria. Even small reductions in input variability translate directly into kiln stability.

Immediate
SCADA alarm

Set a hard SCADA alarm at 1150 °C reduction-zone temperature

Currently treated as a guideline operators try to stay under — making it a hard alarm with logged occurrences would create a measurable accretion-risk indicator.

Short-term
Downtime logging

Begin logging downtime by stage and cause-code

A simple shift-by-shift downtime register would convert the current operator estimate (~40–50% kiln-related) into a measurable Pareto baseline the team could improve against.

Medium-term
ANN soft-sensor

Pilot an ANN-based metallization & accretion predictor

Train a multi-layer-perceptron on existing SCADA history (feed rates, multi-point temperatures, gas composition) cross-referenced with lab metallization. Literature reports R > 0.95 for similar 100 TPD plants. Deploy as a soft-sensor for shift operators.

Long-term
CFD study

CFD-based kiln temperature profile study

Typically run with an academic partner (IIT/NIT). DRI literature on similar 100 TPD plants reports 3–5% metallization gains from optimised inclination/rotation/air-injection configuration.

Reflection

What this internship taught me.

01

Operations is about constraints

A 100 TPD plant doesn't get bigger by improving 5 things 10% each. It gets bigger by fixing the kiln. Pareto and Theory of Constraints both point to the same answer: spend disproportionate effort on the dominant constraint, ignore the rest until that one is solved.

02

SCADA shows; it doesn't predict

The control-room screens display every parameter in real time, but accretion still "sneaks up." The next frontier in DRI ops is predictive — ANN soft sensors + CFD-validated models. This is where MBA-trained operations managers add real value: bringing data-science thinking to a domain that has historically been operator-experience driven.

03

Operator conversations > spreadsheets

The ~40–50% kiln-downtime estimate didn't come from a report. It came from a shift in-charge over tea. The most valuable information in a plant lives in the heads of the people who run it. Structured downtime logging would surface this — but the first hour of any improvement project should be on the shop floor, not in the office.

A note on this study's scope

Observation, not measurement.

This was an observational MBA internship study, not a measured Six Sigma project. The numbers presented are either standard reference values for 100 TPD coal-based DRI plants (from process literature), or estimates given to me casually by the operations team. No proprietary plant data, SCADA exports, or internal reports are reproduced here. Any specific quantitative claim is labelled with its source.
