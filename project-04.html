The Dual-Failure Pattern
In typical inventory management, two failure modes coexist: excess inventory tying up working capital in high holding costs, and periodic stockouts causing production stoppages. Counter-intuitively, both problems can occur simultaneously — the inventory may appear adequate in average terms while still creating shortages due to poor reorder timing.
The EOQ Framework
With three inputs — annual demand (D), ordering cost per purchase order (S), and holding cost per unit per year (H) — the model plots total cost against varying order quantities to identify the minimum: the EOQ point where ordering cost and holding cost are exactly equal.
Why Over-Ordering Persists
Many organisations order in large infrequent batches to chase supplier discounts — which drives up holding cost while also creating long inter-order gaps where stockouts can occur mid-cycle. Ordering more frequently in smaller batches costs marginally more per order but saves far more in holding + stockout costs combined.
Three-Part Solution Structure
EOQ gives the optimal order quantity. ROP (Reorder Point = lead-time demand) tells you when to order. Safety Stock (= z × σ_demand × √lead_time) buffers against demand variability — eliminating stockouts without bloating average inventory. The three work together as one integrated control loop.
EOQ Formula Derivation
Total annual cost = Ordering cost + Holding cost = (D/Q)×S + (Q/2)×H. Minimise by differentiating with respect to Q and setting to zero: dTC/dQ = −DS/Q² + H/2 = 0 → Q* = √(2DS/H). At this point, ordering cost exactly equals holding cost — the economic optimum.

Why the framework matters
The EOQ model works not because it produces a magic number, but because it forces you to precisely quantify the opposing cost forces — ordering and holding — and find the exact point where they balance. Most organisations over-order to avoid stockouts and unknowingly pay 2–3× the optimal holding cost as a result. The framework's value is showing you that excess inventory and stockouts can both be reduced simultaneously by ordering more, smaller batches at the right reorder trigger.
A note on scope
This is a conceptual MBA framework application — a worked example with illustrative numbers, not a case study of a specific organisation's procurement records. The 18% saving figure refers to the worked example shown (Q=1500 vs Q*=632 at D=5000, S=₹200, H=₹5). Use the live calculator above to substitute any real D / S / H values to see the corresponding EOQ for your context. The applied version of this framework — built into a working tool with 12 SKUs for small retail — is Project 07 — InventoryIQ.
