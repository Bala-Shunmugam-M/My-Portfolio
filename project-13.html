Why a rejection is the strongest possible result
A tool that always returns a confident green number is dangerous — it will certify savings that aren't real. On genuine industrial data with only proxy drivers, WattsWorth's out-of-sample error exceeded the IPMVP threshold, its Durbin-Watson statistic correctly flagged the autocorrelation that real data always carries, and it reported the baseline as not yet certifiable. That is the right answer. The system knows the difference between a model that fits and one that is good enough to put money and a carbon claim behind — exactly the judgment an energy auditor is paid for. Refusing to over-claim is the feature.

Architecture

Engine-first: pure analytics, thin UI

The core is a pure-Python, UI-free analytics library. The Streamlit pages, the report generator and the readiness gate are thin skins over it — which is why the same engine runs unchanged on synthetic data, on a real steel plant, or (next) behind an API.

The case

Energy savings are easy to claim and hard to prove

Why it matters
Heavy industry is energy-cost-dominated and now carbon-accountable. India's PAT scheme, BRSR disclosure, and the EU's CBAM (in force from January 2026, hitting ~90% of India's steel exports to Europe) make a verified energy number a board-level, money-and-compliance number.
Why baselines fail
A saving is only as good as its baseline. Most tools fit a flat average, ignore that energy depends on production and weather, and never test statistical validity — producing confident numbers no auditor will sign.
Why M&V matters
IPMVP measurement & verification separates a project's effect from the noise of changing operations. It converts "we think we saved energy" into "we saved X, with this confidence."

Lessons learned

Data quality beats algorithms
The hardest, highest-value part of M&V isn't the regression — it's knowing whether the data can support one. The readiness gate exists because the real failure mode is a confident answer built on inadequate data.
Honest uncertainty beats false precision
A saving with a confidence interval and an autocorrelation-corrected significance — and a refusal to certify when the fit is poor — is more useful, and far more credible, than a single confident number.
Energy analytics is a business problem
Every output is in rupees and tonnes of CO₂, because a plant manager funds a decision, not a kilowatt-hour. The statistics are in service of the P&L.
Scope is part of the work
Stating what the tool cannot do — single-site, daily, no live meter feed — is what makes the claims about what it can do believable.
What's mine, and how AI was used
The methodology and the statistical & validation design are mine: reporting out-of-sample error, correcting significance for autocorrelation, gating the pipeline on data readiness, and blinding the engine to injected ground truth so detection could be measured. So are the architecture and product calls — what to build, what to refuse to build, and where the honest limits are. AI coding assistance accelerated the implementation so the effort went into the analytics and the judgment. The thresholds and the honest-rejection behaviour were chosen deliberately — not by a model.
Results & roadmap

What it does today — and what it doesn't

Today it works end to end: validated ingestion & upload → readiness verdict → ISO 50001 baseline with diagnostics → IPMVP savings in ₹ / tCO₂ with confidence → anomaly detection & forecasting → capex-budget optimization → audit-grade PDF & Excel. Live, public, and reproducible.

What it does not do — honest scope
Single site, daily resolution, CSV / manual data in — no live SCADA / historian feed, no sub-metering hierarchy, no multi-tenant authentication. It is a decision-and-verification layer, not a real-time control system. That is deliberate: real-time telemetry is exactly what the large EMS platforms already do well — the defensible-M&V and data-readiness layer is what they do least well, and where a focused tool adds the most.
Next
Utility-bill ingestion — the one dataset a customer can hand over without IT integration, and the canonical IPMVP Option C input.
Short-term
Locked, version-stamped baselines with a data hash — the provenance that makes M&V auditable over multiple years.
Medium-term
IPMVP fractional savings-uncertainty ratio, and multi-site fleet benchmarking of energy intensity (kWh/tonne).
Long-term
A FastAPI service layer over the existing engine — the architecture already supports it, since the engine is UI-free.
