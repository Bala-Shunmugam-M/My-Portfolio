Context
The data, the questions, the methods
The Dataset
shopping_behavior_updated.csv — a publicly available Kaggle retail dataset of 3,900 customer records. Variables span: demographics (Age, Gender), transaction (Purchase Amount, Category, Item, Season, Location), behavioral (Subscription Status, Discount Applied, Frequency of Purchases, Previous Purchases, Review Rating, Payment Method). Zero missing values.
The Research Questions
Six testable questions: Do men and women spend differently? Does season affect spending? Is gender linked to subscription? What latent factors drive behavior? How do customers cluster? Can we predict who will subscribe? Each answered with a different statistical method and a formal hypothesis test.
Why Python
The R analytics projects (P09-P11) used Quarto for reproducible reporting. This project uses Python's scientific stack — pandas, scipy, sklearn, seaborn, matplotlib — which is the industry-standard environment for ML-adjacent analytics. Both matter for a business analytics role. The Jupyter notebook is fully reproducible.
What I Took Away
The most striking result: zero female customers subscribed in the dataset — only males subscribed (39.7% subscription rate). This drove a Chi-Square statistic of 676.8 (p < 0.001) — the largest test statistic in the study by far. The logistic regression then confirmed discount usage as the biggest predictor of subscription (coefficient 3.37).

What each test found
Section 5.1 · Inferential
Independent T-Test
Does mean purchase amount differ between male and female shoppers?
t = −0.877, p = 0.381
Male: $59.54 · Female: $60.25
FAIL TO REJECT H₀
Gender does not drive spending differences. Gender-neutral pricing is appropriate for this retailer.
Section 5.2 · Inferential
One-Way ANOVA
Does mean purchase amount vary significantly across Spring, Summer, Fall, Winter?
F = 3.746, p = 0.011
Fall: $61.56 · Spring: $58.74
REJECT H₀
Seasonal variation is statistically significant. Fall & Winter spend more than Spring & Summer — justifies seasonal pricing models.
Section 5.3 · Association
Chi-Square Test
Is there a significant association between Gender and Subscription Status?
χ² = 676.79, df = 1, p < 0.0001
Female subscription rate: 0% · Male: 39.7%
REJECT H₀ (strongly)
The most striking finding. Zero female subscribers. Subscription acquisition efforts should be gender-targeted based on this data.
Section 5.3B · Dimensionality
PCA
Can four numeric shopping variables be reduced to fewer dimensions?
2 components retained
Eigenvalues: 1.049, 1.029 (> 1.0)
2 PCs RETAINED
Near-equal eigenvalues (all close to 1.0) indicate the four variables are weakly correlated — no dominant single factor drives shopping behavior.
Section 5.4 · Factor Analysis
Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA)
What latent constructs underlie the numeric shopping variables?
2 factors, Varimax rotation
Factor 1: Age + Prev. Purchases · Factor 2: Amount + Rating
2 FACTORS
Factor 1 (Customer Engagement): purchase frequency and history. Factor 2 (Transaction Quality): purchase amount and satisfaction. Shopping behavior is multidimensional.
Section 5.5 · Segmentation
K-Means Clustering
How do 3,900 customers naturally segment by behavior?
k=3 (elbow method)
Cluster 0: 1,438 (36.9%) · Cluster 1: 1,200 (30.8%) · Cluster 2: 1,262 (32.4%)
3 CLUSTERS
Cluster 0: Young (age 28), avg spend, low purchase history — new shoppers. Cluster 1: Older (age 53), high spend $81.38 — high-value veterans. Cluster 2: Older (age 53), low spend $38.59 — budget-conscious.
Section 5.6 · Prediction
Logistic Regression
Can we predict which customers will subscribe?
AUC-ROC = 0.893
Accuracy = 85% · Top predictor: Discount_binary (coef 3.37)
EXCELLENT DISCRIMINATOR
Discount usage is by far the strongest predictor (coef 3.37) — customers who use discounts are far more likely to subscribe. Gender binary is second (coef 0.75). Age and rating are near-zero.

Discount is the key lever
Discount_binary has a coefficient of 3.37 — exponentially larger than any other predictor. A customer who has used discounts is dramatically more likely to subscribe than one who hasn't. This is actionable: target discount users with subscription conversion campaigns first.
Age and rating barely matter
Age (0.0052) and Review Rating (0.0058) are essentially zero in subscription prediction. A 30-year-old and a 60-year-old with the same discount behavior are equally likely to subscribe. Demographic-only targeting would miss this — behavioral data is what matters.

The interesting contrast: Clusters 1 and 2

These aren't different types of customers in terms of loyalty; they're the same loyalty profile but at different price points. Cluster 2 is not budget-constrained — they're buying lower-value items or using discounts. This is a different intervention than assuming they can't afford more. The high-value veterans (Cluster 1) are the retention priority; Cluster 2 could be migrated upward with targeted product exposure.
