02

Five rules for speaking under pressure

  1. Short sentences win. Every line in this script is 8–15 words. Long sentences are where grammar breaks and panic starts. Pause between sentences instead.
  2. Numbers never lie. "Four classical frameworks integrated into one mobile tool" never fails. "A significantly impactful improvement" can collapse. Memorise the numbers, not the adjectives.
  3. Pauses sound confident. A 2-second pause sounds thoughtful. Filling silence with "um, basically, actually" sounds nervous. Pause, breathe, continue.
  4. Show, don't describe. You're doing a live demo. When the words fail, click the next page. The visual carries 60% of the message.
  5. Recover with structure. If you lose your place, say "Let me show you the result first" and click to the metrics. The numbers reset your flow.
03

The 30-second opener

memorise word-for-word

When they say "Tell me about yourself", this is your answer. Practise until you can say it in your sleep. pause markers show where to breathe.

Good morning. I am Bala Shunmugam. pause

I am an Electrical Engineer pursuing my MBA in Operations and Business Analytics at Amrita School of Business. pause

My background is unusual. pause I have done in-plant training at a gas turbine power station, and I am now learning operations frameworks.

So I can read a single-line diagram, and I can also run an EOQ calculation.

I have built two working tools — an inventory control system and a Six Sigma project manager. pause

I would like to show you the inventory tool first, if that's okay?

Why this works: It states your unique angle (EEE + MBA bridge) in two simple sentences, gives one concrete example each side (single-line diagram + EOQ), shows you ship things (two tools), and ends by giving the interviewer control ("if that's okay?"). Total: ~28 seconds.
If your nerves spike, the safest fallback is to just say the first three sentences and stop. Then ask: "Shall I tell you about my projects?" Giving them a yes/no question buys you time to breathe.
04

Hero — InventoryIQ + Project 07

4 min · always lead with this

This is the only project you are guaranteed to present. Open the InventoryIQ tool on your laptop before the interview starts. Have it pre-loaded with demo data.

Step 1 — Open the tool, then say:

This is InventoryIQ. I designed it for unorganised small retail shops in India. pause

The problem I'm solving: small shop owners run out of fast-selling items, but their money is stuck in slow-moving stock. They order on intuition — when shelves look low.

I built this around a realistic 12-SKU basket modelled on a typical Madurai grocery shop. Rice, oil, sugar, pulses, soaps — the actual items I'd see if I walked into one. pause

Step 2 — Click on a product, then say:

For each SKU, the tool calculates four things automatically.

EOQ — how much to order. pause ROP — when to reorder.

Safety stock — buffer for uncertainty. pause ABC class — is this an A, B, or C priority item?

All four formulas are integrated. The shop owner sees one answer: "Order N units now." pause

Edit any cell — demand, lead time, cost — and watch all four update live. That's the value: four classical frameworks, one tap, one answer.

Step 3 — Show the dashboard, then say:

This is the manager dashboard view — same data, more detail. ABC pie chart, stockout alerts, reorder priorities. pause

What the textbook tells us: small retailers running ad-hoc reordering typically have 30–45% excess inventory and frequent stockouts of their best sellers. EOQ-plus-ROP closes most of that gap.

The tool's value isn't in proving those specific numbers — I haven't deployed it in a real shop yet. The value is in making the textbook math one-tap accessible to someone who has never opened Excel.

Step 4 — Show the mobile view, then close with:

I also designed a mobile version. pause Because the actual shop owner is on a phone in his apron pocket, not a laptop on a desk.

This was my MBA applied research project. pause The concept, the framework integration, the UX — that's mine. The code? I used AI tools to accelerate implementation. I'm comfortable being transparent about that — it's a 2026 reality. What matters is I owned the spec and the design.

Total time: 3.5–4 minutes if you breathe between sentences. Most likely follow-up: "Did you deploy it in a real shop?" Answer honestly: "Not yet — it's a working demonstrator. The next step is piloting with a real outlet." That's defensible. Inventing a deployment is not.

Backup A — VGTPS Operations (Project 02)

2 min · use if asked "what else?"

If the interviewer asks "what other projects?", use this. It shows you can think in OM frameworks.

My next project is a field study at Valuthur Gas Turbine Power Station. pause

I did my in-plant training there in February 2025.

It's a 187 megawatt combined-cycle plant — Phase I with a GE Frame 6FA, Phase II with an Ansaldo unit.

For my MBA operations project, I applied TANGEDCO's own five operational lenses — Operation & Maintenance, Crisis Management, Safety, Reliability, Efficiency. pause

Using the actual multi-year plant performance documents from my training pack.

The most striking finding: cost of generation went from ₹1.32 per kWh in 2004 to ₹22 per kWh in 2020. pause A 17-times swing.

Almost entirely driven by gas price and reduced dispatch. pause

The other pattern I found: every recorded inspection ran 30 to 50 percent beyond OEM-recommended fired hours. The May 2010 stator fault and June 2012 rotor replacement both came out of that pattern.

So my recommendation: tighten the maintenance-deferral discipline first, then add vibration analytics on rotating equipment — because most major outages have been rotating-equipment failures.

Why this works: Real plant data, real failure events, framework from the plant's own training material. If asked "did you do full NPV calculations?" — say honestly: "I framed the recommendations around NPV concepts but didn't do full cash-flow modelling. The capital-ROI examples in my study are the plant's own actual capex-versus-revenue figures." That answer is defensible. Inventing NPV numbers is not.

Backup B — Adaptive Relay (Project 08)

2 min · use if technical interviewer

Use this only if the interviewer is technical (an engineer, not a HR person), OR if they ask about your B.E. final-year project, OR if you sense the conversation needs more engineering credibility.

My final-year B.E. project was on adaptive protection relay coordination. pause

In May 2010, a stator earth fault at Valuthur caused a three-month outage. I learned about it during my in-plant training.

The relay missed the fault because it was high-resistance — small differential current.

Fixed-setting relays can't detect that during peak operating mode. pause

I designed an adaptive four-group 87G differential scheme. pause

It uses real plant data — 51,921 amperes bus fault level, 172 volts stability voltage — from VGTPS documentation.

By the relay characteristic math, the adaptive setting brings the threshold below the fault current. pause

Literature on similar adaptive 87G schemes reports clearance in forty to fifty milliseconds — within IEEE C37.102 guidance.

This was a coordination study, not a Simulink simulation. The analytical case is solid.

I analysed ten fault scenarios across five protection zones.

Why this matters for Schneider / Hitachi / ABB: these companies sell protection relays. When you talk 87G differential, adaptive setting groups, and IEEE C37.102 coordination, you are speaking their product language. If the interviewer asks "did you actually simulate this in Simulink?" — say honestly: "No, it's a coordination study using plant data and the relay characteristic math. I cite literature trip-time figures, not my own simulation." That answer earns respect. Claiming a simulation you didn't run does not.

The closing pitch

30 sec · when they ask "anything else?"

Most candidates stumble when asked "Why should we hire you?" or "Anything else to add?" at the end. Have this ready.

Most MBA candidates can talk about Lean and Six Sigma.

Most engineers can talk about plant operations. pause

I can do both. pause

And I ship working tools, not just reports.

That's what I bring.

Why this is short: at the end of a 15-min interview, energy is low. A 6-line closing lands harder than a 30-line one. The "I ship working tools, not just reports" line is your differentiator — say it slowly.

Likely questions & short answers

prep all of these

The most common follow-ups. Practise saying each answer in under 20 seconds.

About InventoryIQ

Where did the data come from?

The 12 SKUs are realistic examples modelled on a typical Madurai grocery shop — assembled from observation of local outlets, not collected as 30 days of measured sales from one specific shop. The defaults are editable so it works for any small retailer.

Have you deployed it in a real shop?

Not yet. It's a working demonstrator. The next step would be piloting with a real outlet and measuring before-vs-after performance over a quarter.

How did you build the tool?

I designed the spec, the framework integration, and the mobile-first UX. For the implementation I used AI as a code accelerator — HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. The four formulas (EOQ, ROP, Safety Stock, ABC) run in the browser. I'm comfortable being transparent about the AI assistance — what matters is I own the design decisions.

Why integrate four frameworks instead of just EOQ?

EOQ tells you how much to order. ROP tells you when. Safety stock handles uncertainty. ABC tells you which products deserve your attention. A shop owner needs all four — separated, they're a textbook chapter. Integrated, they're a tool.

What improvement could a shop expect?

Inventory literature on EOQ-plus-ROP versus ad-hoc reordering in small retail consistently reports 30–45% inventory value reduction and substantial stockout drops. I haven't measured those numbers myself — that's the next phase. The tool's value today is making the math accessible.

Why not just use Excel?

A shop owner cannot use Excel on a phone between customers. I designed this for the actual user — one tap, one number: "Order N units." That's the UX insight.

About VGTPS

What is VGTPS exactly?

It's a 187.2 MW combined-cycle gas plant in Ramanathapuram, run by TANGEDCO. Two phases: Phase I (commissioned 2002-03) is 95 MW with GE Frame 6FA + BHEL steam turbine. Phase II (commissioned 2008-09) is 92.2 MW with an Ansaldo unit.

What's combined-cycle?

A gas turbine generates power and exhausts hot gas. That hot gas heats water to drive a second turbine — the steam turbine. You get more power from the same fuel. Brayton cycle + Rankine cycle in one plant.

What is evaporative cooling?

It cools the air going into the gas turbine. Cooler air is denser, so the turbine produces more power. It mainly helps in summer when output drops due to ambient heat.

Why does summer reduce output?

Gas turbine output drops by roughly 0.5-0.7 percent for every degree Celsius above ISO conditions (15°C). In Tamil Nadu summer at 35-40°C ambient, that's a major loss compared to nameplate.

Why does Phase I outperform Phase II?

Phase I won the Bronze Shield in 2006-07 at 87.42% PLF. Phase II (Ansaldo) has had multiple long forced outages — 16 months from Jan 2010, 20 months from Jan 2015 — mostly vibration-related rotating-equipment failures. Equipment specification choices at procurement matter for the plant's whole life.

About the Relay project

What is 87G?

87G is the differential protection relay for a generator. It compares the current entering and leaving the generator. Any difference means a fault inside.

Why does the fixed-setting relay miss high-resistance faults?

High resistance limits the fault current. The fixed setting is calibrated for full-current faults, so a small current looks like normal operation to the relay.

What does "adaptive" mean here?

The relay setting changes based on the operating condition. Fixed setting is one number for all situations. Adaptive recalculates the threshold dynamically.

HR / behavioural

Why operations after EEE?

During my plant visits, I saw that the biggest losses were not technical. They were in scheduling, maintenance planning, and inventory. Engineering teaches you the system. Operations teaches you how to run it.

Why Schneider Electric / Hitachi?

Your products are exactly what I studied — protection relays, energy management, motor controls. I want to work where my electrical knowledge becomes a business asset, not a side note.

What is your weakness?

I get nervous in formal interviews. I am working on it by practising aloud and focusing on numbers and short sentences. In one-on-one technical discussions, I am comfortable.

Do you have any questions for us?

Yes — two. First, what does a typical first six months look like for a graduate hire in this role? Second, which projects in your team most use the engineering-plus-operations combination?
